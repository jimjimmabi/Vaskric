Names & Culture

The calendar, the names, and the legacy of the Vaskrin people

The Vaskrin people live in the shadows of history, but their language preserves the memory of their origins. Their calendar, their names, and their concepts of time are all derived from a mixture of ancient Latinate roots and the local East Asian environment they came to inhabit.

I. Days of the Week

The days are named after celestial bodies (sun, moon) and the five visible planets, which are associated with Roman gods. In daily speech, the dierna (day) part is often dropped.

Mardierna 마르디에르나 Tuesday Mars-day
Merkurdierna 메르쿠르디에르나 Wednesday Mercury-day
Jovdierna 조브디에르나 Thursday Jove (Jupiter)-day
Venerdierna 베네르디에르나 Friday Venus-day
Saturnidierna 사투르니디에르나 Saturday Saturn-day
Soldierna 솔디에르나 Sunday Sun-day

III. Personal Names

Vaskric names follow the phonotactic rules (2+ syllables, Latinate roots). They are often derived from virtues, nature, or historical figures. In Vaskoryŏ, these names are transliterated into Hangul, but they keep their Latin roots.

Family Names

Vaskric family names are often descriptive or patronymic, but they follow the same Latinate roots.

  • Valerius (Strong) → 발레리우스
  • Kaelus (Of the sky) → 카엘루스
  • Fortis (Strong) → 포르티스
  • Albus (White) → 알부스
  • Marinus (Of the sea) → 마리누스

IV. Local Toponyms (Sinitic-derived)

While the personal names are Latinate, the names of the villages and surrounding regions in the East are Sinitic-derived, reflecting the actual geography of the Korean peninsula. These are written with Chinese characters (Hanja) or transliterated into Hangul with tone marking.

  • Tánjiācūn — The village
  • Běikǒu — The northern pass
  • Shímén — Stone gate

This contrast—Latinate personal names and Sinitic place names—is a visible trace of the community’s dual heritage.
