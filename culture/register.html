Registers & Sociolinguistics

How history shaped the way Vaskric is spoken

The Vaskrins have been a suppressed minority for much of their history. Suppression took the form of restrictions on language use in public life, mandatory registration with authorities, and periodic campaigns to assimilate or relocate the community. This history produced a strict diglossia — different ways of speaking depending on the social context.

Modern Vaskric operates with three primary registers, plus one historical hidden register.

❧ ❧ ❧

I. The High Register (Liturgical)

This is the language of scripture, formal poetry, legal declarations, and religious ceremonies. It is highly archaic, preserving older Latin-derived forms that have been lost in everyday speech.

  • Word Order: VSO (Verb-Subject-Object). It puts the verb first to emphasize the action (e.g., “Kréavat Deun...” instead of “Deun kréavat...”).
  • Vocabulary: Uses exclusive theological roots like Deun (God), Lúmen (Light), and Ténebra (Darkness).
  • Grammar: Uses the holy conjunction éd instead of the standard e (“and”).
❧ ❧ ❧

II. The Neutral Standard

This is the default grammar described on this website. It is the everyday written language and the formal spoken language used in education, administration, and published prose.

  • Word Order: SVO (Subject-Verb-Object).
  • Vocabulary: The standard Latinate core mixed with established Sinitic loans.
  • Grammar: The full diacritic system is used in writing. In speech, consonants are fully pronounced and the /r/ is trilled.
❧ ❧ ❧

III. The Contact Register (Low Register)

This is the everyday spoken language, heavily shaped by centuries of contact with Cantonese and Mandarin. It is used in rapid, informal speech. It is heavily simplified.

  • Word Order: Topic-comment order. Solar, ek videren (“The sun, I see it”) is the default in rapid speech, not a marked topic.
  • Grammar: Serial verb constructions (Cantonese-style). Optional classifiers with counted nouns (e.g., tri go chā — three [classifier] teas).
  • Phonology: Aspect over tense. Complex onsets simplify (vaskr- → vask-), and final -ŋ neutralizes to -n under Mandarin influence. Reduplication is used for intensity (frigon-frigon — bitterly cold).
❧ ❧ ❧

IV. The Covert Register (Historical)

Historically, a fourth register existed: an in-group way of speaking used to evade monitoring by authorities. It was characterized by euphemism, indirection, and the full use of the lexical diacritic layer in writing.

The diacritic shibboleth is the most famous artifact of this register. Insiders could read a text aloud fluently, but outsiders transcribing the spoken language could not reproduce the written form because the diacritic choice was purely lexical.

Taboo Lexicon

This register developed a small taboo vocabulary for discussing authorities and forbidden topics in public:

Vaskric Literal Euphemistic meaning
l’okren longen the long eye the authorities
l’lingua brefen the short tongue an informant
l’portren apert the open door a raid
l’pulvis the dust disappearance, death
l’silentia the silence a period of suppression
❧ ❧ ❧
A single sentence can be rendered in any of the three registers, and a fluent speaker moves between them without effort. Knowing when to switch is part of knowing the language.
