Vaskoryŏ Texts

Poems, songs, scripture, and narrative in the Eastern Dialect

Language is more than grammar and lexicon. These texts demonstrate Vaskoryŏ in use — across registers, across moods, and across the community’s long and difficult history. They are presented here in the adapted Hangul script, alongside their Standard Vaskric equivalents and English translations.

❧ ❧ ❧

I. The Hangul Poem — Learner’s Refrain

A learner’s refrain, celebrating the adapted script

솔라 비르 루브렌 에 메리카 비르 블라벤. 에크 비데렌 솔라 수프렌 에 메리카 인프렌. 비스 반드렌 메리카로 에 비데렌 솔라. 오만 후리곤 네바스카; 안 에스 도르멘.

Standard Vaskric (Latin)

L’solar bir rubren e l’merika bir blaven. Ek videren l’solar supren e l’merika infren. Vis vandren to l’merika e videren l’solar. L’oman frigon nevaskra; an es dormen.

Translation

The sun is red and the sea is blue. I see the sun above and the sea below. We walk to the sea and see the sun. The cold man does not speak; he is sleeping.

II. The Song — Kor Meum Plorat (My Heart Cries)

A piece written to be sung. It uses a simple, repetitive structure and emotional vocabulary. In Vaskoryŏ, the pronunciation shifts (e.g., kor → 코르, plorat → 플로랏) make it sound distinctly different from the Standard dialect, even though the words are the same.

코르 메움 플로랏 인 르실렌티아, 녹스 에스트 롱가, 세드 에크 스페렌. 룩스 베니트 인 르마티나, 에크 비데렌 르솔라 레벤.   코르 메움 플로랏, 세드 에크 네 티메. 투 에스 메아 아니마, 투 에스 메아 룩스. 셈페르, 에크 테 비데오. 셈페르, 에크 테 아모.   프라길리스 에스, 세드 에크 에스 포르티스. 코르 메움 플로랏, 세드 에크 스페렌. 룩스 베니트 인 르마티나. 에크 비데렌 르솔라 레벤.

Standard Vaskric (Latin)

Kor meum plorat in l’silentia, Nox est longa, sed ek speren. Lux venit in l’matina, Ek videren l’solar leven.   Kor meum plorat, sed ek ne time. Tu es mea anima, tu es mea lux. Semper, ek te video. Semper, ek te amo.   Fragilis es, sed ek es fortis. Kor meum plorat, sed ek speren. Lux venit in l’matina. Ek videren l’solar leven.

Translation

My heart cries in the silence, The night is long, but I hope. Light comes in the morning, I see the sun rise.   My heart cries, but I do not fear. You are my soul, you are my light. Always, I see you. Always, I love you.   You are fragile, but I am strong. My heart cries, but I hope. Light comes in the morning. I see the sun rise.

III. Scripture — Genesis 1:1-3 (High Register)

The High Register in full. In Vaskoryŏ, this passage uses the VSO word order (Verb-Subject-Object) and theological vocabulary, but rendered entirely in the Hangul script.

1. 인 르이니티아, 크레아바트 데운 르카엘룸 에드 르테람.
2. 에드 르테라 에사트 이나니스 에드 바쿠, 에드 테네브라 에사트 수프렌 르파스티아 르아뷔시. 에드 르스피리트 데 데운 에사트 모벤트 수프렌 르파스티아 르아콰레.
3. 에드 디카바트 데운: «에스 루멘!» 에드 루멘 에사트 파케르트.

Standard Vaskric (Latin)

1. In l’initia, kréavat Deun l’kaelum éd l’terram.
2. Éd l’terra esat inánis éd váku, éd ténebra esat supren l’fástia l’abýssi. Éd l’Spirit de Deun esat movent supren l’fástia l’aquare.
3. Éd dicavat Deun: «Es lúmen!» Éd lúmen esat fakert.

Translation

1. In the beginning, God created the heaven and the earth.

2. And the earth was without form and void, and darkness was upon the face of the deep. And the Spirit of God was moving upon the face of the waters.

3. And God said: “Let there be light!” And light was made.

IV. Short Story — The Lost Book

A simple narrative demonstrating the past tense, the passive voice, and the basic SVO syntax of the Neutral Standard register, rendered in Hangul.

인 운 디에르나, 운 오만 반드랏 토 포레스타. 안 포르탓 운 리프렌. 안 에샷 펠리센. 세드 안 카덴 에 리프렌 에샷 페르디텐. 르오만 비드랏 리프렌 인 르아콰레. 안 에샷 트리스텐. 안 네 사피렌 크보 파케렌. 안 달랏 토 르도무스 에 도르미렌.

Standard Vaskric (Latin)

In un dierna, un oman vandrat to l’foresta. An portat un libren. An esat felisen. Sed an kaden e l’libren esat perditen. L’oman vidrat l’libren in l’aquare. An esat tristen. An ne sapiren kvo fakeren. An dalrat to l’domus e dormiren.

Translation

One day, a man walked to the forest. He carried a book. He was happy. But he fell and the book was lost. The man saw the book in the water. He was sad. He did not know what to do. He went to the house and slept.

V. Word-by-Word Comparison

To highlight the systematic phonological shifts between the two dialects, here is a breakdown of the first sentence from the story above:

Vaskoryŏ (Hangul) Standard Vaskric (Latin) English
인 in in
운 un a (one)
디에르나 dierna day
오만 oman man
반드랏 vandrat walked
토 to to
포레스타 l’foresta the forest

Notice how initial v- shifts to b- (vandrat → 반드랏), and the past tense suffix -t is rendered with a final consonant in Hangul (e.g., 랏), which makes it sound like a sharp, unreleased stop, a very Korean phonetic feature.
