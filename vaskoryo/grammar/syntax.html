Vaskoryŏ Syntax

SVO with Korean-influenced contact patterns

Vaskoryŏ syntax is the same SVO, ergative-adjacent, diacritic-driven system as Vaskric’s. Every rule from the Vaskric syntax page applies here unless noted below. What differs is the frequency of certain patterns and the shape of the contact register.

The reason is simple: Korean reshaped the everyday register of Vaskoryŏ the way Mandarin reshaped the everyday register of Vaskric — but the two contact languages pull in different directions. Korean is more strongly topic-comment, more strongly verb-final in subordinate clauses, and more strongly aspect-driven. All of that shows up here.

❧ ❧ ❧

I. The basic SVO order

Unchanged from Vaskric. Subject, then verb, then object.

Vaskoryŏ Hangul English
ek vaskra에크 바스카I speak
tu videren solar투 비데렌 솔라you see the sun
an porten lipren안 포르텐 리프렌he carries the book
vis vandren to domus비스 반드렌 토 도무스we walk to the house

Note the shift in one example: Vaskric libren becomes Vaskoryŏ lipren, because initial l before a vowel has stiffened and the br cluster simplified under the eastern consonant changes.

❧ ❧ ❧

II. Adjective and adverb placement

Unchanged. Adjectives follow nouns; adverbs follow verbs.

Vaskoryŏ Hangul English
solar rupren솔라 루프렌red sun
oman frigon오만 후리곤cold man
domus alpen도무스 알펜white house
❧ ❧ ❧

III. Ergative alignment

Same as Vaskric. The transitive subject takes the acute; the intransitive subject takes the grave; the transitive object takes the diaeresis. In Hangul, these are rendered with the vowel-modifier system described on the Hangul page.

Role Vaskoryŏ (Latin) Vaskoryŏ (Hangul)
Transitive subject (agent) Ék vaskra 에에크 바스카
Intransitive subject (patient) Èk vandren 에크ㆆ 반드렌
Transitive object ek vaskra lünguam 에크 바스카 뤼응과므

The acute becomes a doubled vowel in Hangul; the grave becomes a vowel followed by ㆆ; the diaeresis becomes an ㅇ separator.

❧ ❧ ❧

IV. Questions

Yes/no questions

Same particle ka, sentence-final.

Vaskoryŏ Hangul English
tu vaskra ka?투 바스카 카?do you speak?
an videren solar ka?안 비데렌 솔라 카?does he see the sun?

Wh-questions

Wh-word moves to sentence-initial position. But Vaskoryŏ often shows a Korean-influenced alternative in the contact register: the wh-word stays in place, marked by intonation. This is one of the contact-register differences.

Register Vaskoryŏ English
Neutral kva tu videren? 크바 투 비데렌? what do you see?
Contact tu videren kva? 투 비데렌 크바? what do you see? (colloquial, wh-in-situ)

The in-situ pattern is borrowed from Korean, where the question word stays where its referent would be. Vaskoryŏ uses both, depending on register.

❧ ❧ ❧

V. Topic-comment order

This is where the Korean influence runs deepest. In the Vaskoryŏ contact register, the topic-comment order is not a marked construction — it is the default word order in rapid speech. The topic comes first, then the comment.

Neutral (SVO) Contact (topic-comment) English
ek videren solar 에크 비데렌 솔라 solar, ek videren 솔라, 에크 비데렌 the sun, I see it
an komren panren 안 콤렌 판렌 panren, an komren 판렌, 안 콤렌 the bread, he is eating it

In Vaskric, this order is a marked, emphatic construction. In Vaskoryŏ, it is what you say when you are just talking.

❧ ❧ ❧

VI. Serial verb constructions

Korean-style serial verb constructions appear in the contact register: two or more verbs chained to express a single complex action. This is common in speech about motion and giving.

Vaskoryŏ (Contact) Meaning
ek porten lipren veniren 에크 포르텐 리프렌 베니렌 I bring the book (carry-come)
an dalren videren 안 달렌 비데렌 he goes to see

The neutral standard uses e (“and”) between the verbs. The contact register chains them without a conjunction, Korean-style.

❧ ❧ ❧

VII. Aspect over tense in rapid speech

In the contact register, speakers lean on aspect particles es (progressive) and sol (habitual) and often drop the -t and -l tense suffixes entirely. This is the same phenomenon as Mandarin-influenced Vaskric, but it runs deeper in Vaskoryŏ because the Korean aspect system is so strongly grammaticalized.

Full (Neutral) Reduced (Contact) English
ek es vaskra 에크 에스 바스카 es vaskra 에스 바스카 I am speaking
ek sol vaskra 에크 솔 바스카 sol vaskra 솔 바스카 I used to speak
❧ ❧ ❧

VIII. Negation

Prefix ne- on the verb. Same as Vaskric.

Vaskoryŏ Hangul English
ek nevaskra에크 네바스카I do not speak
an nevideren solar안 네비데렌 솔라he does not see the sun
❧ ❧ ❧

IX. Subordination

Same relative pronoun som, following the noun.

Vaskoryŏ Hangul English
l’oman som vaskra로만 솜 바스카the man who speaks
l’domus som ek videren르도무스 솜 에크 비데렌the house that I see

One Korean-influenced alternative in the contact register: the relative clause can come before the noun, Korean-style. This is unusual and rare, and considered a mark of heavy contact speech.

❧ ❧ ❧

X. The registers of Vaskoryŏ

Unlike Vaskric, Vaskoryŏ has less formal register variation. The three main registers are:

Public register (Korean-dominant)

In public life, the community uses Korean. Vaskoryŏ is not used for administration, education, or mass media. This is the largest single difference from Vaskric, which maintains a functioning public High Register.

Private register (Vaskoryŏ)

The language of the household, the mountain, and the community. This is where Vaskoryŏ lives. It is the register that carries the everyday syntax described on this page.

Sacred register (High)

The High Register is preserved in Vaskoryŏ for scripture and ceremonial use, but it is used far less often than in the west. It is a survival from an older, more unified period, preserved by the community as a mark of identity rather than as a regular liturgical language.

The west speaks Vaskric every day. The east speaks Vaskoryŏ at home, and Korean everywhere else. The High Register is a memory the east keeps for itself.
