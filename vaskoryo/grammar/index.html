Vaskoryŏ Grammar

The same Latinate core, reshaped by Korean contact

Vaskoryŏ grammar is fundamentally the same as Vaskric’s. The morphological type is agglutinative; the alignment is ergative; the verb system is three synthetic tenses plus aspect particles; nouns take a plural suffix but no case marking; the diacritic layer carries syntactic function. The differences are in the details — some syntactic patterns have been pulled toward Korean, some vocabulary has shifted, and the everyday register shows contact phenomena that the western branch does not.

This hub links to the detailed grammar pages. Read them alongside the Hangul page, because the script and the grammar are inseparable in Vaskoryŏ.

Where the east differs from the west

Most of the grammar is identical. Four things change:

1. The future suffix

Vaskric has -r. Vaskoryŏ has -l, because final r becomes l in the east.

Form Vaskric Vaskoryŏ Meaning
Futurevaskrar바스크랄 /baskɨral/will speak

2. The passive auxiliary

Vaskric uses bir. Vaskoryŏ devoices the initial consonant: pir.

Form Vaskric Vaskoryŏ Meaning
Passivebir vaskrat피르 바스크랏 /piɾ baskɨrat/is spoken

3. The contact register

Vaskoryŏ’s everyday register is more Korean-influenced than Vaskric’s everyday register is Mandarin-influenced. Topic-comment order appears more often; serial verb constructions are more common; the aspect particles es and sol appear with higher frequency than in Vaskric.

4. The public/private split

Vaskoryŏ has less formal register differentiation than Vaskric. Where Vaskric preserves a full liturgical High Register (see Vaskric Syntax §X), Vaskoryŏ mostly uses Korean for public and ceremonial language. Vaskoryŏ itself lives primarily in the private sphere.
