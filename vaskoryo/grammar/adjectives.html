Adjectives

Description, comparison, and placement in the Eastern Dialect

In Vaskoryō, adjectives are your best friend. Unlike Latin or Romance languages, adjectives are completely invariant. They do not change for gender, number, or case. You learn one form, and you use it everywhere. The only rule you need to remember is that adjectives come after the noun they describe.

❧ ❧ ❧

I. Placement and Invariability

In English, we say "the red sun." In Vaskoryō, you say "the sun red." The adjective always follows the noun.

Because Vaskoryō has no grammatical gender, an adjective like rubren (red) never changes to rubrena or rubrenum. It stays rubren whether describing a masculine, feminine, or inanimate object.

Vaskoryō Hangul Literal Translation Meaning
solar rubren 솔라 루브렌 sun red red sun
domus alben 도무스 알벤 house white white house
kaniren magnen 카니렌 마그넨 dog big big dog
luna pulchra 루나 풀크라 moon beautiful beautiful moon

Note: Even if the noun is plural, the adjective never changes. domuss alben means "white houses," not "whites houses."

❧ ❧ ❧

II. Comparison (Bigger, Biggest)

To compare two things, Vaskoryō uses the word plus (more) before the adjective, and quam (than) to introduce the second item. This is identical to English "more beautiful than."

Type Vaskoryō Hangul Meaning
Positive magnen 마그넨 big
Comparative plus magnen 플루스 마그넨 bigger / more big
Superlative maxime magnen 막시메 마그넨 biggest / most big

Here is a comparative sentence in action:

  • l'kaniren es plus magnen quam l'felren. (The dog is bigger than the cat.)
  • 르카니렌 에스 플루스 마그넨 크암 르펠렌.
❧ ❧ ❧

❧ ❧ ❧

IV. Adjectives as Nouns (The Neuter)

In Vaskoryō, you can turn an adjective into an abstract noun by adding the suffix -tia (similar to English "-ness" or "-ity").

  • bonen (good) → bonetia (goodness)
  • malen (bad) → maletia (evil)
  • forten (strong) → fortetia (strength)
  • sapien (wise) → sapientia (wisdom)
❧ ❧ ❧

V. Summary of Adjective Rules

  • Placement: Always after the noun (solar rubren = red sun).
  • Invariability: Never changes for gender, number, or case.
  • Comparative: Use plus + adjective + quam.
  • Superlative: Use maxime + adjective.
  • Abstract Nouns: Add -tia to the adjective.
