Vaskoryŏ Verbs

The same Latinate verb system, with Korean-contact shifts

Vaskoryŏ verbs are built on the same architecture as Vaskric’s. Tense is suffix-based (three synthetic forms), aspect is carried by particles, mood by suffixes, voice by an auxiliary. There is no person agreement. The verb is a clean, elegant machine, and the east kept it intact.

What changed is small but distinctive: the future suffix and the passive auxiliary have both undergone the east’s final-consonant shifts. The rest of the paradigm is effectively identical.

❧ ❧ ❧

I. Three synthetic tenses

The present is the unmarked base. Past and future are suffixes. Note the future suffix in Vaskoryŏ: -l rather than Vaskric’s -r.

Tense Suffix Latin Hangul Meaning
Present — vaskra 바스카 speak(s)
Past -t vaskrat 바스크랏 spoke
Future -l (Vaskric -r) vaskral 바스크랄 will speak

The future shift -r → -l is one of the signature differences from Vaskric. It comes from the general east-shift of final r to l, described in the eastern history.

❧ ❧ ❧

II. Aspect

Aspect particles are the same as in Vaskric. The progressive and habitual are more frequent in everyday Vaskoryŏ than in Vaskric, because the contact register leans on aspect over tense.

Aspect Particle Latin Hangul Meaning
Perfect ha ha vaskrat 하바스크랏 has spoken
Progressive es es vaskra 에스 바스카 is speaking
Habitual sol sol vaskra 솔 바스카 used to speak
❧ ❧ ❧

III. Mood

Imperative

Bare stem. Plural imperative adds -te.

Form Hangul Meaning
vaskra!바스카!speak!
vaskrate!바스크라테!speak! (to several)
nevaskra!네바스카!do not speak!

Conditional

Suffix -ev.

Form Hangul Meaning
ek vaskrev에크 바스크레브I would speak
si ek vaskrev...시 에크 바스크레브...if I were to speak...

Subjunctive

Suffix -isse, used after si in hypothetical clauses and after verbs of wanting or emotion.

Form Hangul Meaning
si ek vaskrisse시 에크 바스크리쎄if I were to speak
ek vol som tu vaskrisse에크 볼 솜 투 바스크리쎄I want you to speak
❧ ❧ ❧

IV. Voice

Passive formed with auxiliary pir plus the past participle. This is the second east-shift: Vaskric bir → Vaskoryŏ pir, by the general devoicing of initial b in this auxiliary.

Vaskoryŏ Hangul Meaning
l’libren pir skribrat 립렌 피르 스크리브랏 the book is written
l’oman pir vidrat 로만 피르 비드랏 the man is seen
❧ ❧ ❧

V. Modals

Same five auxiliaries as Vaskric.

Modal Meaning Example
kancan, be ableek kan vaskra — I can speak

maymay, be allowedan may veniren — he may come
shalshall, shouldvis shal vandren — we should walk
volwantek vol vaskra — I want to speak
❧ ❧ ❧

VI. The four irregular verbs

The same four irregular verbs as Vaskric — esen, haven, volen, poden — with the same irregular patterns. Only the surface forms have shifted where the east’s consonant changes touch them.

1. Esen — to be

Form Hangul Meaning

es에스is / am / are (present)
esat에삿was / were (past)
esal에살will be (future — -r → -l)

2. Haven — to have

Form Hangul Meaning

3. Volen — to want

Form Hangul Meaning
volen볼렌to want (infinitive)
vol볼wants / want (present)

4. Poden — to be able / can

Form Hangul Meaning

VIII. Two verb classes

Same as Vaskric. Class I is the regular pattern; Class II is a small closed set of contracting verbs.

IX. Negation

Form Hangul Meaning
ek nevaskra에크 네바스카I do not speak
ek nevaskrat에크 네바스크랏I did not speak
ek nevaskral에크 네바스크랄I will not speak
❧ ❧ ❧
Two changes, and one word — pir instead of bir, vaskral instead of vaskrar. That is the east in a sentence. The grammar is the same. The sound has moved.
