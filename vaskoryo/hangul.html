The Hangul Adaptation

How Korean letters were reshaped to write a Latinate language

Vaskoryŏ uses Hangul as its primary script, adapted systematically to represent Vaskric phonology and morphology. The Korean alphabet — with its scientific, feature-based design — was a natural fit for a community that needed a script of its own. The Vaskoryŏns adopted it, and modified it to carry the Latinate core of their language.

This is one of the two great script differences from the Standard dialect. Where Vaskric uses Latin + diacritics, Vaskoryŏ uses Hangul + its own modifier layer. Both scripts are phonetic to read; both are lexical to write. The visual difference is total. The underlying principle is identical.

The five Latin base vowels map one-to-one to Hangul vowel letters, plus one Korean-influenced addition (ŏ /ʌ/).

The addition of ŏ /ʌ/ is a genuinely Vaskoryŏn feature. Vaskric has no such vowel. It appears in words like 바스코려 (vaskoryŏ), and its presence is one of the small sounds that marks the two dialects apart.

Why digraphs?

Korean has no native /f/ or /v/ phoneme. Rather than borrow a whole new letter, Vaskoryŏ uses a two-letter combination that preserves the distinction:

  • /f/ — ㅎㅍ (ㅎ + ㅍ). Both consonants contribute to the fricative quality.
  • /v/ — ㅂㅎ (ㅂ + ㅎ). Same principle, voiced pairing.
  • /ʃ/ — 시 (ㅅ + 이). The vowel 이 palatalizes the ㅅ.
  • /ɲ/ — 니 (ㄴ + 이). The vowel palatalizes the ㄴ.

These digraphs are the most visible Korean-influenced adjustments in the script. They signal the deep borrowing that shaped Vaskoryŏ in the first place.

IV. The diacritic layer, adapted

Standard Hangul cannot carry Vaskric’s morphological diacritics directly. The Vaskoryŏns solved this with two strategies: modified vowel letters for quality distinctions, and separator marks for hiatus and boundary.

Vaskric mark Vaskoryŏ Hangul strategy Example
acute (´) Doubled vowel á → 아아
grave (`) Vowel + ㆆ à → 아ㆆ
circumflex (̂) Long-vowel marker (ː) â → 아ː
diaeresis (¨) ㅇ separator aï → 아이
tilde (˜) Final ㅇ (nasal) ã → 앙
macron (¯) Tone numeral chā¹ → 차¹

The lexical asymmetry is preserved. A reader can pronounce any Vaskoryŏ word they see. But which diacritic mark a word carries is still lexical — the Hangul spelling of a word still must be learned, not derived from sound alone. This is the same signature difficulty as Vaskric, in a very different visual form.

❧ ❧ ❧

VI. Parallel with Vaskric

A single Vaskoryŏ sentence in Hangul, next to its Latin-script Vaskric equivalent:

Script Sentence
Vaskoryŏ (Hangul) 바스코려 — 바스코려
Vaskric (Latin) vaskryŏ
Vaskric (Cyrillic) васкрич

Three scripts, one word. The Hangul form carries the vowel shift /ʌ/ that Vaskric lacks; the Cyrillic form is the administrative script; the Latin form is the native script of Vaskric. All three are phonetic to read. Only the Hangul and Latin forms carry the lexical diacritic layer.

❧ ❧ ❧
The Hangul adaptation is not a compromise — it is a claim. It says: this language belongs to its place, and its place shaped its script. A Vaskoryŏn reader does not think of Korea when reading Vaskoryŏ. They think of the mountains, the long winters, and the words their grandmother used.
