Vaskoryŏ /vaskoɾjʌ/

바스코려 · Eastern Dialect · the Korean branch

The Vaskoryŏns are the eastern branch of the Vaskrin diaspora. They broke away from the main Vaskrin community during the Mongol invasions and settled in the Korean peninsula’s mountainous interior. There they remained isolated for centuries, surrounded by Korean-speaking communities.

Crucially, the Vaskoryŏns never lost contact with their linguistic roots. They preserved the Latinate core vocabulary, the grammatical system, and the diacritic principle — but their phonology, prosody, and some syntax were profoundly reshaped by Korean contact. The result is a dialect that sounds Korean but is recognisably Vaskric to any Standard Vaskric speaker who listens carefully.

This is the key difference from the Central dialect: Vaskoryŏ is not a separate language but a sister dialect. A Standard Vaskric speaker and a Vaskoryŏn speaker can converse with modest effort after a few days of exposure. The relationship is akin to Spanish and Portuguese, or Danish and Swedish — distinct but mutually intelligible.

Sound changes at a glance

Where Vaskric retains the Latinate form, Vaskoryŏ has drifted:

Feature Vaskric Vaskoryŏ
Final voiced stops /b d g/ kept devoiced to /p t k/
Initial r /r/ → /n/
Final r /r/ → /l/
Complex onsets vaskr-, frigon- kept simplified: vaskr- → bask-
New vowel — ŏ /ʌ/ (as in 바스코려)
Where Vaskric keeps a word’s Latinate skeleton intact, Vaskoryŏ wears the marks of its Korean centuries — softer onsets, cleaner codas, and a new vowel that has no Latin ancestor at all.
