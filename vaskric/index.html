Vaskric /ˈvaskritʃ/

васкрич · Standard Dialect · the Western branch

The Vaskrins are the western branch of the Vaskrin diaspora — a small, historically suppressed minority living in rural communities outside what is now Beijing. Their ancestors were Roman-era settlers who reached East Asia along trade routes and were cut off from the Mediterranean world by the collapse of long-distance contact. Over centuries of isolation they preserved a Latinate core, but their language was profoundly reshaped by the surrounding Sinitic languages and by a deliberate, systemic divergence from their ancestral tongue.

The result is a language that looks French on the page, sounds Latin in the mouth, and carries its history in its diacritics — a writing system that is easy to read aloud but deliberately hard for outsiders to transcribe from speech.

Feature Vaskric
Family Latinate core, Sinitic-reshaped
Script Latin + productive diacritic layer (primary); Cyrillic (contact, phonetic)
Consonants 20 native (plus loan-only affricates)
Vowels 5 base (a e i o u) + diacritic modifications
Stress Penultimate by default
Alignment Ergative — transitive subjects acute, intransitive subjects grave, objects diaeresis
Word order SVO (standard), VSO (High Register)
Registers High (scripture) · Neutral Standard · Contact (Sinitic-influenced)
Signature Phonetic to read, lexical to write — the inverse of French
❧ ❧ ❧
The paradox, stated plainly: Vaskric looks like French on the page — same diacritic palette, same elision marks, same hyphenated compounds — but when read aloud it sounds like a Latin-derived language with full final consonants, trilled /r/, penultimate stress, and no silent letters. A French speaker would recognise the shape of the words but not understand a single one by ear.
