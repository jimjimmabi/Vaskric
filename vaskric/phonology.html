Vaskric Phonology

The sound system — consonants, vowels, and the shape of syllables

The native consonant inventory is Latin-derived, but has undergone a systemic shift. The vowel system is compact — five base vowels — and the real complexity lies in the diacritic layer (see Diacritics) and in the phonotactic rules that constrain how sounds can combine.

Vaskric has 20 native consonants, a five-vowel base system, and a small loanword stratum (from Mandarin and Cantonese) that adds a handful of affricates not found in native roots.

II. The Great Consonant Shift

Vaskric’s consonant system diverged from Latin by a set of four systemic shifts. These are the changes that turned Latin into Vaskric.

Latin Vaskric Example
kw p Latin quattuor → Vaskric patwar
gw b Latin lingua → Vaskric limba
f- h- (word-initially) Latin filius → Vaskric hilus
s before i, e š /ʃ/ Latin sine → Vaskric šine

These four shifts are why Vaskric looks so alien to a Latin reader despite sharing the bulk of its vocabulary. The sounds are different, but the semantic roots are recognisably related.

❧ ❧ ❧

III. Vowels

Vaskric has five base vowels. Every other vowel sound in the language is produced by applying a diacritic to one of these five.

IV. Phonotactics

Syllable template: (C)(C)V(C) — up to two consonants in onset, exactly one coda.

Legal onsets (two consonants)

pr, br, tr, dr, kr, gr, fr, vr, pl, bl, kl, gl, fl, sl, sp, st, sk, sm, sn, sv

Legal codas (single)

p, b, t, d, k, g, f, v, s, z, š, m, n, ñ, ŋ, l, r

No triple clusters in onset or coda. If affixation would create one, an epenthetic -e- is inserted: vaskr + -s → vaskras (not vaskrs).

Stress

Penultimate by default. Marked with the acute only when irregular.

❧ ❧ ❧

V. Register-dependent simplification

In the Contact Register — the everyday spoken register shaped by centuries of Sinitic contact — two rules apply in rapid speech:

  • Complex onsets simplify. vaskr- → vask-. A speaker in fast conversation may say vaskra → vaska.
  • Final -ŋ neutralises to -n. Under Mandarin influence, final ŋ collapses to n.

The High Register preserves the full clusters intact.

❧ ❧ ❧

VI. The tapped /r/

Vaskric’s /r/ is a single tap of the tongue against the alveolar ridge — identical to Korean ㅈ in the middle of a word, or Spanish pero. It is not the English approximant, and not the French uvular /ʁ/.

If you can say Korean 사랑 (sarang) or Spanish pero, you can already pronounce Vaskric’s /r/.
