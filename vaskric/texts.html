Vaskric Texts

Poems, songs, scripture, and everyday dialogues

Language is more than grammar and lexicon. These texts demonstrate Vaskric in use — across registers, across moods, and across the community’s long and difficult history. Read them aloud. The Latin core, the French-style diacritics, and the full final consonants all come together here.

I. Poem — Semper, Ek Te Video

Always, I See You

A poem of deep connection, reassurance, and the feeling of being understood without words. Uses the poetic fronting rule from Syntax, the emotional vocabulary from the lexicon, and the High Register’s Latinate possessive forms.

Nox est longa, sed ek ne time. Ténebra esat supren, sed tu es mea lux. Semper, ek te video. Semper, kor meum est tuum.   Fragilis es, sed ek es fortis. Timor venit, sed ek semper adest. Ne angoressa. Respira cum me.   Lux in tenebris, tu es. Anima mea, tu es. Semper, ek te amo. Semper, ek te video.

Translation

The night is long, but I do not fear. Darkness is above, but you are my light. Always, I see you. Always, my heart is yours.   You are fragile, but I am strong. Fear comes, but I am always here. Do not overthink. Breathe with me.   You are the light in the darkness. You are my soul. Always, I love you. Always, I see you.

Notes on the register

This poem uses mea (my) and meum (my) instead of the standard Vaskric mé — a deliberate archaism borrowed from Latin for poetic weight. Est (is) replaces the standard es. These are the kinds of choices the High Register makes available for emotional or liturgical texts.

II. Song — Kor Meum Plorat

My Heart Cries

A song of longing and hope. Repetitive structure, simple vocabulary, a refrain that returns. Suitable for a slow, melodic tune.

Kor meum plorat in l’silentia, Nox est longa, sed ek speren. Lux venit in l’matina, Ek videren l’solar leven.   Kor meum plorat, sed ek ne time. Tu es mea anima, tu es mea lux. Semper, ek te video. Semper, ek te amo.   Fragilis es, sed ek es fortis. Kor meum plorat, sed ek speren. Lux venit in l’matina. Ek videren l’solar leven.

Translation

My heart cries in the silence, The night is long, but I hope. Light comes in the morning, I see the sun rise.   My heart cries, but I do not fear. You are my soul, you are my light. Always, I see you. Always, I love you.   You are fragile, but I am strong. My heart cries, but I hope. Light comes in the morning. I see the sun rise.

Rhythm: 4/4 time, 60–70 BPM. Melancholic but hopeful. Piano, guitar, or strings.

III. Diary — A Day in Winter

A personal, reflective text. Uses everyday vocabulary, emotional expressions, and the progressive/habitual aspects.

Text

Decembra 12, Annera 2026

Dierna esat longen. Ek vaken in l’matina e ek sentiren un ponderen in l’kordun. Ek ne sapiren kvi. Ksk ek es fatiren de l’anneras, o ksk es l’noktra de l’hiemra.

Ek vandren to l’fenren e ek videren l’nival kaden. L’mund esat alben e silentia. Ek pensiren de l’tempora kvan ek esat un filren. L’tempora kvan ek timiren l’tenebra. Sed nun, ek ne timoren. Ek sapiren l’tenebra esat part de l’lumen.

Ek prenden un kafé e ek skriberen in l’libren. Ek skriberen de l’somnium — l’somnium de un domus in l’montan, kun un fokren e un kordun plen de amoren. Ek skriberen de l’vasking e l’lingua de l’populen.

L’solar leven e l’nival sesiren. Ek videren l’lumen sur l’fenren e ek sentiren un speren in l’kordun. L’anno esat novel, e kun l’anno veniren l’somnium.

Translation

December 12, Year 2026

The day was long. I woke in the morning and I felt a weight in my heart. I do not know why. Perhaps I am tired of the years, or perhaps it is the night of winter.

I walked to the window and I saw the snow falling. The world was white and silent. I thought of the time when I was a child. The time when I feared the darkness. But now, I do not fear it. I know the darkness is part of the light.

I took a coffee and I wrote in the book. I wrote of the dream — the dream of a house in the mountains, with a fire and a heart full of love. I wrote of the speaking and the language of the people.

The sun rose and the snow ceased. I saw the light on the window and I felt a hope in my heart. The year is new, and with the year comes the dream.

Vaskric English
L’solar bir rubren e l’merika bir blaven. The sun is red and the sea is blue.
Ek videren l’solar supren e l’merika infren. I see the sun above and the sea below.
Vis vandren to l’merika e videren l’solar. We walk to the sea and see the sun.

VII. Scripture — Genesis 1:1–3

The High Register in full. VSO word order, the holy conjunction éd, prophetic fronting, and the archaic theological vocabulary.

  1. In l’initia, kréavat Deun l’kaelum éd l’terram.
  2. Éd l’terra esat inánis éd váku, éd ténebra esat supren l’fástia l’abýssi. Éd l’Spirit de Deun esat movent supren l’fástia l’aquare.
  3. Éd dicavat Deun: «Es lúmen!» Éd lúmen esat faker.

Translation

  1. In the beginning, God created the heaven and the earth.
  2. And the earth was without form and void, and darkness was upon the face of the deep. And the Spirit of God was moving upon the face of the waters.
  3. And God said: “Let there be light!” And light was made.

Notes

  • Kréavat Deun — VSO: verb first, then subject. The High Register fronts the verb for emphasis.
  • Es lúmen! — bare imperative of esen (to be) + lúmen (light). Literally: “Light be!”
  • Esat faker — passive: past of esen + past participle faker.
  • Éd — the holy conjunction, replacing ordinary e (“and”).

VIII. Colloquial — The Contact Register

Everyday rapid speech. Topic-comment order, serial verbs, optional classifiers, Sinitic loans. Compare with the Neutral Standard above.

Contact Register Neutral Standard English
Solar, ek videren. Ek videren solar. The sun, I see it.
Tri go chā, ples. Tri chā, ples. Three teas, please.
Ek porten libren veniren. Ek porten libren e veniren. I bring the book (carry-come).
The same language, at different speeds, in different rooms — formal in the temple, conversational in the kitchen, and both unmistakably Vaskric.
