Vaskric Verbs

Tense, aspect, mood, voice — and the absence of person agreement

The Vaskric verb is the simplest part of the grammar and the most elegant. Verbs never inflect for person or number — the pronoun carries all of that. What the verb carries is tense (three synthetic forms), aspect (particles), mood (suffixes), and voice (auxiliary + participle). Once you have seen the full paradigm of vaskren “to speak”, you have seen every regular Vaskric verb.

This page is the reference. For the origin of the system, see History; for how verb phrases combine into sentences, see Syntax.

❧ ❧ ❧

I. Three synthetic tenses

The present is the unmarked base. Past and future are suffixes.

Tense Suffix Example IPA Meaning
Present — vaskra /vaskra/ speak(s)
Past -t vaskrat /vaskrat/ spoke
Future -r vaskrar /vaskrar/ will speak

That is the complete tense system. Every Vaskric verb follows this pattern unless listed as irregular (see below).

❧ ❧ ❧

II. Aspect

Aspect is carried by particles that precede the verb. Three aspects are standard:

Aspect Particle Example Meaning
Perfect ha ha vaskrat has spoken
Progressive es es vaskra is speaking
Habitual sol sol vaskra used to speak / speaks habitually

The progressive and habitual particles are mandatory in the Contact Register and common in the Neutral Standard whenever aspect matters more than tense. In fast speech they often substitute for the past and future suffixes entirely: es vaskra “is speaking” rather than vaskrat “spoke.”

❧ ❧ ❧

III. Mood

Imperative

Bare verb stem, no suffix. The plural imperative adds -te.

Form Meaning
vaskra!speak!
vaskrate!speak! (to several)
nevaskra!do not speak!

Conditional

Suffix -ev.

Form Meaning
ek vaskrevI would speak
si ek vaskrev...if I were to speak...

Subjunctive

Suffix -isse, used after si in hypothetical clauses and after verbs of wanting or emotion.

Form Meaning
si ek vaskrisseif I were to speak
ek vol som tu vaskrisseI want you to speak
❧ ❧ ❧

IV. Voice

The passive is formed with the auxiliary bir plus the past participle (verb root + -t). The agent, when stated, is introduced by by.

Form Meaning
l’libren bir skribratthe book is written
l’oman bir vidratthe man is seen
l’libren bir skribrat by l’omanthe book is written by the man
❧ ❧ ❧

V. Modals

Five monosyllabic auxiliaries precede the main verb. They are not inflected for tense; time is carried by context or by the particle before them.

Modal IPA Meaning Example
kan/kan/can, be ableek kan vaskra — I can speak
must/must/musttu must dalren — you must go
may/maj/may, be allowedan may veniren — he may come
shal/ʃal/shall, shouldvis shal vandren — we should walk
vol/vol/wantek vol vaskra — I want to speak
❧ ❧ ❧

VI. The four irregular verbs

Only four verbs are irregular, and all four are the oldest roots in the language. Every other verb follows the pattern of vaskren.

1. Esen — to be

Highly suppletive: each tense has its own root.

Form IPA Meaning
esen/esen/to be (infinitive)
es/es/is / am / are (present)
esat/esat/was / were (past)
esar/esar/will be (future)

2. Haven — to have

Used primarily as the auxiliary for the perfect tense, but also as a main verb.

Form IPA Meaning
haven/haven/to have (infinitive)
ha/ha/has / have (present)
hat/hat/had (past)

3. Volen — to want

Irregular stem in the present tense.

Form IPA Meaning
volen/volen/to want (infinitive)
vol/vol/wants / want (present)
volit/volit/wanted (past)

4. Poden — to be able / can

Same irregular pattern as volen.

Form IPA Meaning
poden/poden/to be able (infinitive)
pod/pod/can / is able (present)
podit/podit/could / was able (past)
❧ ❧ ❧

VII. The full paradigm of vaskren

Every regular verb in Vaskric conjugates exactly like this. Learn this table and you have the verb system.

Form IPA Meaning
vaskren/vaskren/to speak (infinitive)
vaskra/vaskra/speak(s) (present)
vaskrat/vaskrat/spoke (past)
vaskrar/vaskrar/will speak (future)
ha vaskrat/ha vaskrat/has spoken (perfect)
es vaskra/es vaskra/is speaking (progressive)
sol vaskra/sol vaskra/used to speak (habitual)
vaskrev/vaskrev/would speak (conditional)
vaskrisse/vaskrisse/were to speak (subjunctive)
bir vaskrat/bir vaskrat/is spoken (passive)
vaskra!/vaskra/speak! (imperative)
vaskrate!/vaskrate/speak! (plural imperative)
vasking/vaskiŋ/speaking (gerund)
nevaskra/nevaskra/does not speak
kan vaskra/kan vaskra/can speak
vol vaskra/vol vaskra/wants to speak
❧ ❧ ❧

VIII. Two verb classes

Vaskric distinguishes two verb classes by how they form the past tense and past participle.

Class I — Regular (the vast majority)

Past = stem + -t. If the stem ends in a consonant that would create an illegal cluster, an epenthetic -e- is inserted.

Infinitive Past / Participle
vaskrenvaskrat
viderenvideret
portenportet

Class II — Contracting (small, closed)

Verbs ending in an unstressed vowel undergo contraction when the past suffix -t is added.

Infinitive Past / Participle Meaning
dicendictto say
facenfactto make / do
ducenductto lead

The -er forms (diceren, faceren) are formal-register variants of the shorter dicen, facen — not a separate class.

❧ ❧ ❧

IX. Negation

Negation is the prefix ne- attached to the verb, before the tense suffix.

Form Meaning
ek nevaskraI do not speak
ek nevaskratI did not speak
ek nevaskrarI will not speak
