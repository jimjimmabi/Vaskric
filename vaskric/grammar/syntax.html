Vaskric Syntax

Word order, ergative alignment, questions, and the three registers

Vaskric is an SVO language, but its alignment is ergative — and that combination is what makes the syntax distinctive. The subject of an intransitive verb behaves like the object of a transitive verb, not like the subject of a transitive verb. The diacritic layer marks the difference, and the reader who does not attend to it will constantly misparse sentences.

Below: the basic order, the ergative pattern, question formation, subordination, and the three registers that shape real speech.

❧ ❧ ❧

I. Basic word order — SVO

In the Neutral Standard, the sentence order is Subject – Verb – Object.

Vaskric IPA English
ek vaskra/ek vaskra/I speak
tu videren solar/tu videren solar/you see the sun
an porten libren/an porten libren/he carries the book
vis vandren to domus/vis vandren to domus/we walk to the house

Adjective placement

Adjectives follow the noun.

Vaskric English
solar rubrenred sun
oman frigoncold man
domus albenwhite house

Adverb placement

Adverbs follow the verb.

Vaskric English
an vaskra prokselhe speaks nearby
vis kurren distalwe run far
❧ ❧ ❧

II. Ergative alignment

Vaskric is an ergative language. The single argument of an intransitive verb behaves like the object of a transitive verb, not like the subject of a transitive verb. In Vaskric the distinction is carried by diacritics on the pronoun and on the content words.

Role Diacritic Example Meaning
Transitive subject (agent) acute (´) Ék vaskra I speak [transitive]
Intransitive subject (patient) grave (`) Èk vandren I walk [intransitive]
Transitive object diaeresis (¨) ek vaskra l̈nguam I speak the language

For the full diacritic system, see the Diacritics page. The point here is the syntax: the same word ek takes three different marks depending on its role.

Why this matters

In an ergative system, the reader cannot assume that the “subject” of an intransitive sentence is the same kind of thing as the “subject” of a transitive one. Vaskric marks this on the words themselves, which is why a Vaskric sentence without diacritics is ambiguous.

In Vaskric, case is not a property of the sentence. It is a property of every content word in it — and the reader must be attentive to every mark.
❧ ❧ ❧

III. Negation

Negation is the prefix ne- attached to the verb.

Vaskric IPA English
ek nevaskra/ek nevaskra/I do not speak
an nevideren solar/an nevideren solar/he does not see the sun
❧ ❧ ❧

IV. Questions

Yes/no questions

Sentence-final particle ka.

Vaskric IPA English
tu vaskra ka?/tu vaskra ka/do you speak?
an videren solar ka?/an videren solar ka/does he see the sun?

Wh-questions

The wh-word moves to sentence-initial position. The verb stays in place.

Vaskric English
kva tu videren?what do you see?
kver an dalren?where does he go?
kvan tu veniren?when do you come?
kvi an nevaskra?why does he not speak?
❧ ❧ ❧

V. Articles

Definite article

l’ before vowels; l before consonants, written joined to the next word.

Vaskric IPA English
l’oman/lʔoman/the man
l’solar/lʔsolar/the sun
ldomus/ldomus/the house

Indefinite article

un before all words.

Vaskric English
un omana man
un solara sun
❧ ❧ ❧

VI. Subordination

Relative clauses are introduced by som and follow the noun.

Vaskric English
l’oman som vaskrathe man who speaks
ldomus som ek viderenthe house that I see

Embedded clauses keep SVO order, introduced by som or a conjunction.

Vaskric English
ek sapiren som an vaskraI know that he speaks
ek pensiren som tu venirenI think that you are coming
❧ ❧ ❧

VII. Coordination

e “and”, o “or”, ma “but”.

Vaskric English
ek vaskra e tu audirenI speak and you listen
tu veniren o tu dalren?are you coming or going?
❧ ❧ ❧

VIII. Topic and focus

Fronting a constituent marks topic. No particle is needed; word order alone signals the move.

Vaskric English
solar, ek viderenthe sun, I see (it)
ek, ek vaskraas for me, I speak
❧ ❧ ❧

IX. Pro-drop

Subject pronouns may be omitted when clear from context, because the verb does not inflect for person and the context disambiguates.

Full Pro-drop English
ek vaskravaskraI speak
tu vaskravaskrayou speak
vis vaskravaskrawe speak

Pro-drop is optional, not obligatory. In formal writing the pronoun is usually kept.

❧ ❧ ❧

X. The three registers

Vaskric syntax varies with register. The same idea can be expressed three ways.

High Register — VSO

Scriptural, liturgical, archaic. Verb first, then subject, then object. The holy conjunction éd replaces e. Prophetic fronting is common. See the Texts page for full examples.

High Register Neutral
Kréavat Deun l’kaelumDeun kréavat l’kaelum

Neutral Standard — SVO

Everyday written and formal spoken Vaskric. This is the default grammar described on this page.

Contact Register — topic-comment

Everyday spoken, especially rapid or informal. Sinitic-influenced. Topic-comment order, serial verbs, optional classifiers, aspect over tense, cluster simplification. See the History page, section II, for the full list of features.

Neutral Contact
ek videren solarsolar, ek videren
ek porten libren e venirenek porten libren veniren
A single sentence can be rendered in any of the three registers, and a fluent speaker moves between them without effort. Knowing when to switch is part of knowing the language.
