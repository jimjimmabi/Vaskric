Nouns & Adjectives

Pluralization, possession, and description in the Eastern Dialect

Vaskoryŏ nouns are remarkably simple compared to the verbs. There is no grammatical gender (unlike Latin or French), and there is no case marking (unlike German or Russian). Nouns are marked only for plural and possession. Adjectives are completely invariant; they never change to match the noun they describe.

II. Pluralization

To make a noun plural, you simply add the suffix -s. This is identical to English and Vaskric.

Note: If a noun already ends in -s in the singular (like domus), the plural simply adds another -s. In rapid speech, these often merge into a long hiss /sː/.

❧ ❧ ❧

III. Possession (Owning)

To say "X's Y" (like "the man's dog"), Vaskoryŏ does not use an apostrophe. Instead, it uses the preposition de (of). The possessor comes after the possessed item.

Vaskoryŏ (Latin) Vaskoryŏ (Hangul) Literal Translation Meaning
l'kaniren de l'oman 르카니렌 데 로만 the dog of the man the man's dog
l'domus de ek 르도무스 데 에크 the house of me my house
l'librens de tu 리프렌스 데 투 the-books of you your books

The word de never changes, regardless of the gender or number of the noun. This is much simpler than the Latin genitive case.

❧ ❧ ❧

IV. Adjectives

Adjectives in Vaskoryŏ are completely invariant. They do not change for gender, number, or case. An adjective always comes after the noun it describes (unlike English, but identical to French and Spanish).

Vaskoryŏ Hangul Meaning
solar rubren 솔라 루브렌 red sun
domus alben 도무스 알벤 white house
domuss alben 도무스 알벤 white houses

Notice how rubren (red) and alben (white) stay exactly the same whether the noun is singular or plural. You only change the noun.

❧ ❧ ❧

V. Subject and Object (Ergative Alignment)

In Vaskoryŏ, the role of the noun in the sentence (who is doing the action vs. who is receiving it) is marked by diacritics (or in Hangul, by vowel modification). This is the signature difficulty of the language.

This is explained in full detail on the Diacritics page, but here is a quick reminder:

Role Example (Hangul) Meaning
Transitive Subject 에에크 바스카 I speak (something)
Intransitive Subject 에크 ㅇ 반드렌 I walk
Transitive Object 바스카 뤼용과므 speak the language
❧ ❧ ❧

VI. Gender and Animate/Inanimate

Vaskoryŏ has no grammatical gender. There is no "masculine", "feminine", or "neuter" for inanimate objects. The sun (solar) is not masculine, and the moon (lunara) is not feminine.

However, Vaskoryŏ does distinguish between animate (living) and inanimate (non-living) nouns in one specific context: the question word who vs. what.

Question Vaskoryŏ (Latin) Vaskoryŏ (Hangul) Used for...
Who? kva 크바 People, animals
What? kvo 크보 Objects, concepts

Otherwise, nouns are treated exactly the same regardless of whether they are alive or dead.

❧ ❧ ❧

VII. Summary of Noun Rules

  • Plural: Add -s.
  • Possession: Use de after the noun (e.g., domus de ek = my house).
  • Adjectives: Come after the noun, never change for plural.
  • Gender: None.
  • Subject/Object: Marked by diacritics (acute, grave, diaeresis).
