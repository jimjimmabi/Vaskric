Compare the Dialects

One language, two voices

Though they sound vastly different and use different scripts, Vaskric and Vaskoryŏ are fundamentally the same language. They share a Latinate core, an identical grammatical structure, and a common history. The divergence is a story of geography, contact, and survival.

These pages provide a direct comparison to show exactly what changed and what stayed the same.

The Three Faces of Vaskric

The Vaskric language family is often described as having three "faces," depending on the historical trajectory and contact language:

Feature Standard Vaskric Vaskoryŏ (Eastern) Contact Register (West)
Script Latin + diacritics Adapted Hangul Cyrillic (phonetic)
Phonology Latin-sounding, full final consonants Korean-sounding, devoiced final stops Russian-influenced, fully phonetic
/r/ Alveolar trill Tap/flap (Korean ㄹ) Trill
Case marking Diacritics on vowels/consonants Diacritics adapted into Hangul Dropped (replaced by word order)
Word order SVO (VSO in High Register) SVO + optional topic-comment SVO
Lexicon Latinate + Sinitic loans Latinate + Korean calques Latinate + Russian loans
Intelligibility — High (one week to adjust) Moderate
❧ ❧ ❧
A Standard Vaskric speaker and a Vaskoryŏn speaker can meet, struggle for a day, and then converse. That is the measure of a dialect: different enough to be beautiful, same enough to be family.
